\begin{abstract}
\noindent Discrete diffusion language models (DLMs) offer parallel generation but suffer from slow inference due to iterative denoising. We propose \textbf{Entropy-Guided Distillation (EGD-DLM)}, which accelerates DLM inference through knowledge distillation guided by token-level entropy. Our key insight is that high-entropy tokens represent ``hard'' knowledge requiring more attention during distillation. Experiments on WikiText-103 demonstrate \textbf{8.96$\times$ speedup} while the student surprisingly \textbf{surpasses the teacher} in perplexity (1193.30 vs. 1207.28)---a phenomenon not observed in standard distillation.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Diffusion Language Models, Knowledge Distillation, Entropy Scheduling

\section{Introduction}

Autoregressive (AR) models like GPT \citep{radford2019language} and LLaMA \citep{touvron2023llama} dominate NLP but suffer from sequential generation and the ``reversal curse'' \citep{berglund2023reversal}. Discrete Diffusion Language Models (DLMs) such as MDLM \citep{sahoo2024simple}, SEDD \citep{lou2024discrete}, and LLaDA \citep{nie2025llada} enable parallel generation but require 50-1000 denoising steps, creating inference bottlenecks.

\textbf{Related Work.} D3PM \citep{austin2021structured} introduced discrete diffusion with structured transitions. MDLM simplified this using masked diffusion. For acceleration, consistency models \citep{song2023consistency} and progressive distillation \citep{salimans2022progressive} reduce steps for continuous diffusion. SDTT \citep{deschenaux2024sdtt} applies self-distillation for discrete models. Entropy has been used in Diffusion-EAGS \citep{han2024diffusion} for sampling-time scheduling, but not for training-time optimization.

\textbf{Contributions.} We propose EGD-DLM with: (1) \textbf{Entropy-Scheduled Training}: weight loss by token entropy to focus on hard tokens; (2) \textbf{Entropy-Guided Distillation}: use teacher entropy to prioritize knowledge transfer; (3) \textbf{Empirical Validation}: 8.96$\times$ speedup with student surpassing teacher.

\section{Method}

\subsection{Discrete Diffusion Preliminaries}

Following LLaDA \citep{nie2025llada}, let $\mathbf{x}_0 \in \mathcal{V}^L$ be a token sequence. The forward process masks each token with probability $t$:
$q(\mathbf{x}_t | \mathbf{x}_0) = \prod_{i=1}^{L} [ t \cdot \mathbf{1}_{[\texttt{MASK}]} + (1-t) \cdot \mathbf{1}_{[x_0^i]} ]$.
The reverse process trains $p_\theta$ to predict masked tokens with loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{base}} = \mathbb{E}[ \sum_{i: x_t^i = \texttt{MASK}} -\log p_\theta(x_0^i | \mathbf{x}_t, t) ]$.

\subsection{Entropy-Scheduled Training}

We weight loss by normalized token entropy $\tilde{H}^i = H^i / \log |\mathcal{V}|$:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{ES}} = \mathbb{E}\left[ \frac{\sum_{i} w^i \cdot \ell^i}{\sum_{i} w^i} \right], \quad w^i = 1 + \lambda_{\text{train}} \cdot \tilde{H}^i
\end{equation}
where $\ell^i = -\log p_\theta(x_0^i | \mathbf{x}_t, t)$. The entropy is detached to prevent trivial minimization.

\subsection{Entropy-Guided Distillation}

For distillation from teacher $p_T$ to student $p_S$, we weight KL divergence by teacher entropy:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{EGD}} = \mathbb{E}\left[ \sum_{i} w_T^i \cdot \text{KL}(p_T^i \| p_S^i) \right], \quad w_T^i = 1 + \lambda_{\text{distill}} \cdot \tilde{H}_T^i
\end{equation}
The total loss combines soft and hard targets: $\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \alpha \tau^2 \mathcal{L}_{\text{EGD}} + (1-\alpha) \mathcal{L}_{\text{hard}}$.

\textbf{Theoretical Motivation.} Standard KL treats all tokens equally, but high-entropy tokens contain nuanced knowledge harder to transfer. Entropy weighting implements curriculum learning within distillation. Additionally, high-entropy predictions provide implicit label smoothing \citep{muller2019does}, potentially explaining why students can surpass teachers.

\section{Discussion}

\textbf{Why Student Surpasses Teacher.} We hypothesize: (1) High-entropy predictions provide implicit regularization via softer targets; (2) Entropy weighting focuses learning on nuanced distinctions; (3) Distillation may filter noise by emphasizing consistent patterns.

\textbf{Limitations.} (1) PPL $\sim$1200 is higher than AR models (GPT-2: $\sim$30), reflecting DLM training challenges; (2) 71M parameters---larger scale validation needed; (3) Only language modeling evaluated.

\textbf{Future Work.} Scale to LLaDA-8B; learn adaptive $\lambda$; progressive multi-step distillation; downstream task evaluation.

\section{Conclusion}

We presented EGD-DLM, achieving 8.96$\times$ speedup while uniquely enabling students to surpass teachers in perplexity. This work opens new directions for efficient diffusion-based text generation.
